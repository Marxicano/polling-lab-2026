New here? What do LV, RV and these averaging methods mean? Tap for explanations

R+6.0 means the Republican candidate leads by six percentage points in the polls, not that they have a 6% chance of winning. D+3.0 means the Democratic challenger leads by three points. In Nebraska, I denotes an independent candidate.

LV — likely voters: People a pollster's screening or turnout model estimates will vote. Different firms use different screens. RV — registered voters: People registered to vote, whether or not they'll participate. A — adults: Adults surveyed, including people not registered; other labels may denote firm-specific populations.

30-day simple average

One equal vote per included poll released/fielded in the latest 30-day window; uses the three most recent polls if fewer are available. Multiple releases by one firm may count more than once.

Frozen v19

The separately preserved original November 2 snapshot. Not recalculated using subsequent releases.

Latest per pollster

Only the most recent qualifying survey from each firm, so repeated releases by the same pollster don't dominate.

Likely voters only

Uses only LV-tagged surveys from the same window, excluding RV polls. It doesn't prove LV screens are accurate.

Recency/sample weighted

Weights newer polls more heavily (14-day half-life) and also uses the square root of reported sample size or effective sample size when available. Missing effective n is a limitation.

Exclude partisan sponsors

Removes polls flagged as partisan-sponsored, from either side. An unflagged sponsor is not proof of neutrality.

House-effect adjusted

Starts from the latest release per firm and subtracts that firm's estimated relative lean compared with other pollsters; this is not a proven error correction.

Symmetric pollster trim

Removes three firms with the largest Democratic-relative and three with the largest Republican-relative house effects. An exploratory stress test.

Exclude recall-weighted firms

Excludes firms confirmed to use recalled 2024 vote in weighting; firms with unknown methods remain. A sensitivity check, not proof of bias.

Important: Poll margins are not election results. A candidate's reported margin of error describes sampling uncertainty, not the model's total possible election error. None of these methods or scenarios is a calibrated win probability.

What do these numbers mean?
D+3 means the Democratic challenger averages 3 points ahead. I+3 means the independent challenger averages 3 points ahead. R+3 means the Republican averages 3 points ahead. They are polling estimates—not results.
