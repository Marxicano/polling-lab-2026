Historical calibration: facts versus transfer assumptions

We do not apply a universal correction based on historical errors. AAPOR's observed Senate mean signed errors differ between years: the 2022 surveys on average measured the Democratic margin 1.2 points too low; the 2024 surveys measured it 2.4 points too high. Applying either signed error to a 2026 race is a hypothetical transfer, not a validated model. Our 2025 gubernatorial comparisons are different offices and states and are included only as stress tests.

Trump's October 2026 approval in Pew's survey was 30%, versus 38% in early October 2018. That is relevant context for electoral participation but cannot be converted directly to a particular Senate margin. See Pew 2026.

State Navigate's Total Recall Bias study estimates 1–2 point generic-ballot effects under particular recalled-2024-vote weighting assumptions. That is not a universal Senate correction. Our on-screen toggle does not stack a separate recall effect with historical error; it uses one user-selected shift to avoid double counting.

Sources, reproducibility and limits

The 15-state baseline and five methods are embedded verbatim from v19's original frozen snapshot; the alternative no-partisan averages are its existing scenario rows. The seven NYT/Siena comparisons come from Claude's post-freeze sensitivity CSV. The standalone calculator does not contact servers and does not update on its own. This is experimental companion research: it was created after the freeze and cannot be treated as a pre-registered forecast.

An authentic prospective probabilistic model would need election-outcome backtesting, empirically estimated state-specific error distributions, pollster-level house effects, correlated errors, turnout modeling calibrated against validated data, and a published pre-election version. Those requirements remain incomplete. Source repository and frozen public record.
